The challenge

Most organisations still run their most document-heavy processes by hand.

Correspondence arrives by email, web form, post and front desk. Invoices arrive by email, PDF, portal and Peppol. Someone opens each one, keys the details, routes it, chases an approver, and files it for audit. Multiply that across a year and the cost is substantial — not in software, but in people's time, delayed payments and avoidable risk.

The problem compounds quietly. Volumes grow, the team doesn't, compliance obligations tighten, and the organisation absorbs the strain until something breaks. Automation doesn't just cut cost — it removes a structural constraint on how you scale.

  • Manual correspondenceCustomer, supplier and staff email lands in shared inboxes and is triaged by hand. SLAs slip, enquiries get lost between teams, and no one can prove what was actioned.
  • Manual invoice handlingEvery invoice touched by hand is time your finance team isn't spending on higher-value work — and keying errors flow downstream into payments and reporting.
  • Approval bottlenecksInvoices stall waiting on delegated sign-off. Cycle times blow out, early-payment discounts are missed, and suppliers chase status.
  • Records & complianceWhen capture is inconsistent, proving what was received, actioned and retained becomes a scramble — and records-management obligations go unmet.
  • Exceptions & riskDuplicates, mismatches and fraudulent invoices are hard to catch by hand. A single missed duplicate can cost more than a year of automation.

Peppol e-invoicing

A registered Peppol Access Point in both Australia and New Zealand

Instead of a supplier emailing a PDF that someone re-types, a Peppol e-invoice travels as structured data straight from the supplier's system into yours — across a secure, standardised network overseen by the ATO as Australia's Peppol Authority. Fewer errors, faster payment, materially less fraud. We add operational depth on top of basic send-and-receive: review, approval, PO matching, supplier validation, formatting and archival. We also reformat data for accounting systems that cannot accept raw Peppol invoices — removing a common adoption barrier.

The registrations are held by eCloud Business Services Pty Ltd in Australia and Power Business Services Ltd in New Zealand — both trading as Exedee.

  1. Since 1 July 2022

    All Non-Corporate Commonwealth Entities have had to be able to receive Peppol e-invoices — the baseline capability now expected across government buyers.

  2. From 1 July 2026

    At least 30% of the invoices those agencies receive must arrive via e-invoicing. This target is now in effect, and it is reshaping what suppliers expect of every public-sector buyer.

  3. By December 2026

    Agencies must be able to automatically process and send e-invoices, with quarterly progress reported to the Australian Peppol Authority.

What this means depends on where you sit

  • If you supply government

    Peppol capability is fast becoming a condition of doing business.

  • If you are a public-sector buyer

    The milestones above apply to you directly, and the clock has already started.

  • If you are a private-sector organisation

    The same rails cut cost and error even where adoption is entirely voluntary.

We make the connection either way — and wire it into the same automated workflow, so a compliant e-invoice doesn't just arrive, it flows straight through to payment.

Before you book

The questions people ask us first

Do we have to replace our finance, DMS or records system?

No — and that is the point. We are a specialist operational automation provider, not a broad enterprise suite. We automate around your existing finance, ERP, CRM and records platforms and integrate into them, so there is no migration and no re-platforming. Your prior investment is preserved.

How long does it take to go live?

Our accounts payable platform has a proven staged implementation methodology that can be designed, configured, tested and operational within six to nine weeks when delivered as a staged service — which lets the initial stage go live in a short period and the rest follow.

Digitisation and correspondence deployments are scoped the same way: a pilot on a live subset first, then scale. Timelines depend on document mix, integration complexity and your own availability for configuration decisions.

Our documents are messy and inconsistent. Will this still work?

That is the normal starting point. Our extraction is agnostic to format and layout — there is no template to build and maintain for each supplier or document type — and it reads at a stated accuracy of 99%+ across formats.

Quality assurance then runs electronically on both the scanned image and the extracted data, and line-item and total calculations are checked for discrepancies. Anything that fails validation is held back for a person to review rather than passed on incomplete.

Is this only for large organisations?

Not by headcount — by volume and complexity. The determining factor is whether you handle enough documents, with enough manual handling in the workflow, that the cost is measurable. A mid-sized organisation processing thousands of invoices or correspondence items a month is often a better fit than a much larger one whose flows are already automated.

What about compliance, audit and data sovereignty?

Delivered as a cloud-native managed service hosted in Australia in tier-1 data centres and supported locally across Australia and New Zealand, with an optional hot-backup service available. A detailed system-generated audit log records every key user and system action against each document, including field-level changes with the user's name and timestamp — and the ability to roll a field back to its prior contents.

Our eGovernment framing exists specifically because public-sector requirements around network security, data sovereignty and records governance are more stringent, and we deploy against them regularly.
